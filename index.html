Why does RRHO deviate?

Same temperature, same pressure, two worlds. On the left the molecules do not know the others exist. On the right they interact through the intermolecular potential.

RRHO describes molecular degrees of freedom, but does not explicitly include intermolecular interactions. Its gap to the real gas is the missing interaction term, and it closes when the pressure falls or the temperature rises.

Engineering consequence

A heater takes methane from T₁ to T₂. The duty is the area under Cp(T), so the difference between the Cp curves becomes megawatts.

Defaults: 1 kmol/s of methane, 25 to 370 °C at 30 bar, a steam-reformer feed preheater.

Model validity. Shomate correlations describe ideal-gas standard-state heat capacity and have defined validity ranges. RRHO is an idealized molecular model. Real-gas behavior requires appropriate non-ideal corrections or reference data. Here the real gas is the Peng-Robinson equation of state (Tc 190.56 K, Pc 45.99 bar, ω 0.0114): an engineering model, not experimental data. It reproduces the NIST reference Cp of methane at 30 bar within about 0.5 % between 300 and 620 K. The moving molecules are a two-dimensional Lennard-Jones sketch (ε/k 148 K, σ 3.73 Å) with spacing compressed for display; the numbers on this page come from the thermodynamic models, not from the animation.
